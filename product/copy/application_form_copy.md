# Application Form Copy

## Purpose

Draft wording for the application form fields, helper text, and completion messaging.

## Form framing

This form should feel:

- serious
- calm
- selective
- respectful
- not corporate

It should make clear that:

- this is a real cofounder-selection process
- the current priority is the **CTO / Pragmatic Builder** track
- the goal is not to collect polished self-descriptions
- the goal is to surface evidence, judgment, and closure

## Top-of-form intro

### Title

**Grassroot Hopper Cofounder Application**

### Intro copy

This is not a vanity form and it is not a generic hiring workflow.

The current priority is finding a close-the-loop technical cofounder: someone who can turn strong opening moves into finished products people can actually rely on, make senior technical calls, and eventually lead developers.

The form takes roughly **20-30 minutes** if you answer seriously.

Concrete examples matter more than polished language.

## Section: Role track

### Section intro

Pick the role track that best fits how you would actually contribute.

Current review priority is the **CTO / Pragmatic Builder** path.

### Field

**Which role track are you applying for?**

Options:

- CTO / Pragmatic Builder
- Community / Ops Architect

### Helper text

Do not choose based on prestige. Choose based on the work you would realistically own.

## Section: Basics

### Section intro

Enough context to know who you are and where to find your work. No unnecessary formality.

### Fields

**Full name**  
Helper: Use the name you actually want Julien to know you by.

**Email**  
Helper: This is only for this cofounder selection process.

**Location / timezone**  
Helper: Short answer is enough.

**Links to relevant work**  
Helper: GitHub, products, writing, talks, portfolio, demos, event pages, anything that helps show what you have actually done.

## Section: Why now

### Section intro

This section is about real pull, not vague curiosity.

### Field

**What about Grassroot Hopper makes you want to apply now, specifically?**

### Helper text

Avoid generic founder fascination. Explain why this project, this timing, and this role make sense to you.

## Section: Evidence and fit

### Section intro

This is the core of the application. Use real examples. If something was unfinished, say so. If something shipped but in a rough form, say that too.

### Core fields

**What is the strongest proof that you ship? What was your actual contribution, and did it actually get finished or used?**  
Helper: We care about closure, not just output. A smaller finished thing is better evidence than a grand unfinished one.

**Tell me about a time a tool or system didn't do what you wanted, so you bypassed, broke, or rebuilt it.**  
Helper: Show initiative, not just opinion.

**If we build for 3 months and the market ignores it, what do you do Monday morning?**  
Helper: We are looking for reaction under ambiguity, not motivational slogans.

**What AI trend is overrated and why?**  
Helper: Independent judgment matters more than hype fluency.

**Tell me about a time you challenged a strong opinion from a founder, lead, or group. What happened?**  
Helper: Show useful backbone without ego theater.

**Describe a project where the brief was messy or incomplete. How did you create clarity and move it forward?**  
Helper: Tell the story concretely.

**What kind of founder partnership do you work best in, and what kind tends to fail?**  
Helper: This matters. Be honest.

**What level of time and risk can you actually commit in the next 6 months? Be concrete.**  
Helper: Reality is better than ambition theater.

**Tell me about a time you made something simpler instead of more impressive.**  
Helper: We value simplification.

**What is the most boring repeated task in early-stage company building that you would automate first here, and why?**  
Helper: This is partly about leverage and partly about taste.

## CTO / Pragmatic Builder track section

### Section intro

For this track, the key question is not just "can you build?" It is: **do you close loops, own technical direction, and act like a future CTO rather than just a strong developer?**

### Fields

**Show a product, system, script, automation, internal tool, or repo that demonstrates how you work. What trade-off did you make to ship fast, and what made it actually finished enough to matter?**  
Helper: Repo count alone is not impressive. Explain where the thing crossed from interesting to usable.

**When have you built with unclear requirements and missing resources, but still produced something useful and carried it far enough that other people could rely on it?**  
Helper: We care about usefulness and follow-through.

**Tell me about a time the exciting prototype phase was over and the work became slower, denser, or more boring. How did you keep moving until it was done?**  
Helper: This is one of the most important questions in the form.

**Describe a product or system that became more complex over time. What did you do to keep it coherent once the novelty wore off?**  
Helper: We are looking for product maturity under load.

**Tell me about a senior technical decision you owned that affected other builders or the product direction. How did you make the call, and how did you get people aligned?**  
Helper: This is about technical judgment and leadership, not just personal preference.

**If Grassroot Hopper had three developers in a year, what kind of CTO would you be for them?**  
Helper: We are looking for technical ownership and management instinct, not just coding strength.

## Community / Ops Architect track section

### Section intro

This track matters, but it is not the current first bottleneck.

### Fields

**Tell me about a workshop, event series, community, or operational system you helped run. How did you get people to show up or stay engaged?**  
Helper: Concrete ownership matters.

**Describe a time you created structure for a messy human process. What changed after your intervention?**  
Helper: Focus on outcomes, not just effort.

## Section: Self-report context

### Section intro

This part is lightweight context, not diagnosis.

### Helper copy

Answer quickly and honestly. These answers are used to shape judgment and interview follow-up, not to let a machine declare who you are.

## Section: Commitment and constraints

### Section intro

Early-stage collaboration breaks when people hide constraints. Clarity is better than performance.

### Suggested fields

**Availability in the next 6 months**

**Current work situation**

**Financial / risk constraints**

**Preferred collaboration mode**

### Helper text

Short, direct, concrete answers beat optimistic vagueness.
